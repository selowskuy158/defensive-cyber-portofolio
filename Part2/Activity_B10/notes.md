# B10 - Participate in a Cybersecurity Activity as Part of Job/Internship/Volunteering etc.

**Date:** 10 April, 2026

## Summary
This activity involves participating in a cybersecurity related activity through volunteering. I volunteered to help with a cybersecurity awareness workshop organised by the UWA Computer Science and Software Engineering club (CSSE) for first-year students who had little to no knowledge about cybersecurity. The workshop aimed to introduce basic cybersecurity concepts and help students protect themselves online.

## Volunteering Details

### UWA CSSE Cybersecurity Awareness Workshop
- **Date:** 5 April, 2026
- **Location:** UWA CSSE Lab, Perth
- **Role:** Workshop Volunteer/Assistant
- **Duration:** 2 hours

### What I Did
As a volunteer, I helped set up the workshop environment and assisted the main presenter during the session. My main responsibility was to help attendees with the hands-on exercises where they practiced identifying phishing emails and setting up two-factor authentication on their accounts. I walked around the room answering questions from students who were having trouble following the instructions and helped them understand concepts like what makes a password strong and why using the same password on multiple websites is dangerous.

I also prepared a short demonstration showing how easy it is to crack simple passwords using a dictionary attack tool on a controlled local environment. This demonstration really caught the attention of many students because they could see in real time how their common passwords like their pet name or birthday could be guessed within seconds.

### What I Learned
Through this volunteering experience, I learned that teaching cybersecurity to others is a very effective way to strengthen your own understanding of the concepts. I also realized that many university students do not know basic security practices like enabling two-factor authentication or using a password manager, which means there is a real need for more cybersecurity awareness programs at universities. This experience also improved my communication skills because I had to explain technical concepts in simple terms that non-technical students could understand.
