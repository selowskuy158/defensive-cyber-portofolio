# B5 - Attend 2 Cybersecurity Related Talks/Seminars

**Date:** 8 April, 2026

## Summary
This activity involves attending two cybersecurity related talks or seminars to learn from experts and gain insights into current cybersecurity trends and challenges. I attended two different events that covered different aspects of cybersecurity, both of which helped me understand the practical side of the industry beyond what is taught in the classroom.

## Seminars Attended

### 1. UWA Cybersecurity Guest Lecture - "The Future of AI in Cybersecurity"
- **Date:** 20 March, 2026
- **Location:** UWA Engineering Lecture Hall, Perth
- **Speaker:** A cybersecurity consultant from a Perth-based security firm
- **Summary:** The guest lecture focused on how artificial intelligence is being used both by defenders and attackers in the cybersecurity space. The speaker explained that AI can be used to automate threat detection and respond to incidents faster than humans can, but at the same time attackers are also using AI to create more sophisticated phishing emails and malware that can adapt to avoid detection. One interesting point was that the speaker mentioned how deepfake technology is becoming a serious concern for businesses because attackers can use AI-generated voice and video to impersonate executives and trick employees into transferring money or sharing sensitive data. I found this talk very informative because it connected what I am learning in CITS2006 with real-world applications in the industry.

### 2. Perth Cybersecurity Meetup - "Lessons from Recent Australian Data Breaches"
- **Date:** 2 April, 2026
- **Location:** Online via Zoom (organised by Perth Cybersecurity Community)
- **Speaker:** Two panelists from the Australian Cyber Security Centre and a local MSP
- **Summary:** This online meetup discussed the recent wave of data breaches affecting Australian organisations including the Optus and Medibank incidents and what lessons companies should learn from them. The panelists highlighted that many breaches could have been prevented with basic security practices like multi-factor authentication, regular patching, and employee training. They also discussed the Australian government response including the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme and proposed changes to privacy legislation that would increase penalties for companies that fail to protect customer data. I asked a question about how universities can better protect student data, and the panelists suggested that educational institutions should invest more in security awareness training and conduct regular penetration testing of their systems.
