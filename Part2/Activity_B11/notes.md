# B11 - Talk to 2 Cybersecurity Experts from the Industry and Find Out Their Latest Projects

**Date:** 11 April, 2026

## Summary
This activity involves speaking with two cybersecurity professionals from the industry to learn about their work and latest projects. I had the opportunity to talk with two different people working in cybersecurity during the Perth Cybersecurity Networking Night event and through a connection made via LinkedIn.

## Expert Conversations

### Expert 1: SOC Analyst at a Perth Mining Company
- **Name:** (kept anonymous for privacy)
- **Role:** Security Operations Centre Analyst
- **Company:** A major WA mining company

I spoke with this SOC analyst at the Perth Cybersecurity Networking Night event. He explained that his main job is to monitor security alerts and events across the company entire network infrastructure using a SIEM (Security Information and Event Management) platform. His latest project involved implementing a new automated incident response workflow that uses playbooks to automatically contain threats when certain conditions are met, such as automatically isolating a computer from the network if malware is detected on it. He said this automation reduced their average response time from 45 minutes to under 5 minutes for common types of incidents. He also mentioned that one of the biggest challenges in the mining industry is securing OT (Operational Technology) systems that control physical equipment, because many of these systems are old and were not designed with cybersecurity in mind.

### Expert 2: Penetration Tester at a Cybersecurity Consulting Firm
- **Name:** (kept anonymous for privacy)
- **Role:** Senior Penetration Tester
- **Company:** A cybersecurity consulting firm based in Perth

I connected with this penetration tester through LinkedIn after the networking event and we had a conversation over a video call. She explained that her job involves legally breaking into client systems to find vulnerabilities before real attackers do. Her latest project was a red team engagement for an Australian financial services company where her team simulated a full cyberattack including social engineering, network exploitation, and physical security testing over a period of two weeks. She said one of the most interesting findings was that they were able to gain initial access to the company network by dropping USB drives in the parking lot that contained a payload, and two employees plugged them into their work computers. She emphasized that the human element is always the weakest link in security and that technical controls alone are not enough to protect an organisation.
