# B14 - Teach Your Friends About Cybersecurity Topic of Your Choice

**Date:** 12 April, 2026
**Location:** UWA Campus, Perth

## Summary
This activity involves teaching friends about a cybersecurity topic to help spread awareness and understanding of cyber threats. I chose to teach my friends about password security and the importance of using a password manager, because I noticed that most of my friends use weak passwords and reuse them across multiple accounts, which is one of the most common security risks for regular people.

## Teaching Session Details

### Topic: Password Security and Password Managers
I organized a casual teaching session with four of my friends during a study break at UWA. I started by explaining why password security matters and how attackers can use techniques like brute force attacks, dictionary attacks, and credential stuffing to break into accounts with weak or reused passwords.

### Demonstration
I showed my friends a live demonstration using an online tool that estimates how long it would take to crack different passwords. We tested several common password patterns and the results were very eye-opening. A password like "password123" could be cracked in less than a second, while a random 16-character password with mixed characters would take millions of years. This really surprised my friends because some of them admitted to using very similar simple passwords on their accounts.

### Password Manager Introduction
After the demonstration, I introduced the concept of password managers and specifically showed them how Bitwarden works because it is free and open-source. I walked them through creating an account, generating strong random passwords, and how the browser extension automatically fills in login details on websites. I explained that with a password manager, they only need to remember one strong master password and the manager handles everything else.

### Results
By the end of the session, three out of four friends actually installed Bitwarden on their phones and browsers and started migrating their passwords. The fourth friend said they would do it later that night. They were all surprised at how easy it was to use a password manager and wondered why they had not started using one sooner. This teaching experience showed me that people are often willing to improve their security practices when someone takes the time to explain the risks and show them practical solutions.
