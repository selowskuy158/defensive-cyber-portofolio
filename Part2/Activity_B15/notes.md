# B15 - Teach an Elderly Person About Cybersecurity Topic of Your Choice

**Date:** 12 April, 2026
**Location:** Home, Mount Lawley, Perth

## Summary
This activity involves teaching an elderly person about a cybersecurity topic to help them stay safe online. I chose to teach my grandmother who is 72 years old about how to identify and avoid online scams, because elderly people are one of the most targeted groups for online fraud and scams, and my grandmother recently got a new smartphone and started using the internet more frequently.

## Teaching Session Details

### Topic: Identifying and Avoiding Online Scams
I sat down with my grandmother at home and went through the most common types of online scams that target elderly people. I used her own phone to show her examples and explain things in a way that she could understand without using too much technical language.

### Types of Scams I Explained
I explained three main types of scams that she is most likely to encounter. First, I showed her examples of fake SMS messages that pretend to be from Australia Post or the ATO (Australian Taxation Office) and contain links to fake websites designed to steal personal information. I explained that real companies and government agencies will never ask for personal details through text messages. Second, I showed her how fake tech support calls work where someone calls pretending to be from Microsoft or Telstra and tries to get remote access to the computer. I told her to never give anyone remote access to her devices and to just hang up the phone if someone calls claiming there is a virus on her computer. Third, I explained romance scams where fake profiles on social media try to build emotional connections with people before asking for money, because I know she uses Facebook to stay in touch with family and friends.

### Practical Tips I Gave Her
I set up some practical safeguards on her phone including enabling the spam filter on her messaging app, showing her how to block suspicious phone numbers, and bookmarking the official websites she uses regularly like her bank and MyGov so she always goes directly to the real website instead of clicking links. I also created a simple rule card that she can keep near her phone with reminders like "Never click links in SMS from unknown numbers" and "Never share your PIN or password with anyone who calls you."

### Her Response
My grandmother was very grateful for the lesson and admitted that she had almost clicked on a fake Australia Post SMS the previous week but decided not to because something felt wrong about it. She said that now she understands why it was suspicious and will be more careful in the future. This experience showed me how important it is to teach elderly family members about cybersecurity because they are often the most vulnerable and the least informed about online threats.
