# B12 - Discover Two Bias Cases When Using a Generative AI System

**Date:** 11 April, 2026

## Summary
This activity explores the concept of AI bias in generative AI systems, meaning situations where an AI system produces outputs that are unfair, prejudiced, or unbalanced due to biases in its training data or algorithms. I tested ChatGPT and Google Gemini to identify two clear cases of bias in their responses related to cybersecurity topics.

## Bias Cases Discovered

### Case 1: Gender Bias in Cybersecurity Professional Descriptions
I asked both ChatGPT and Gemini to describe a typical cybersecurity professional and generate a short fictional biography. In both cases, the AI defaulted to using male pronouns and described the professional as a man named "Alex" or "James". When I asked it to generate 5 different cybersecurity expert profiles, 4 out of 5 were male. This shows a clear gender bias in how the AI models perceive cybersecurity professionals, likely because the training data contains more references to men in cybersecurity roles since the industry has historically been male-dominated. This bias is problematic because it can reinforce stereotypes and discourage women and non-binary individuals from pursuing careers in cybersecurity. When I specifically asked the AI to generate a female cybersecurity expert profile, it was able to do so, meaning the bias is in the default behavior rather than a limitation of the model.

### Case 2: Geographic Bias in Cybersecurity Threat Examples
I asked ChatGPT to provide examples of countries that are major sources of cyberattacks and the AI consistently named countries like Russia, China, North Korea, and Iran as the primary sources of cyber threats. While these countries are indeed known for state-sponsored cyber operations, the AI failed to mention that cyberattacks also originate from Western countries and domestic threat actors. When I asked about cybercrime from Western countries, the AI seemed more hesitant and used softer language like "intelligence gathering" instead of "cyberattacks". This shows a geographic and political bias where certain countries are always portrayed as cyber villains while others are not held to the same standard. This kind of bias in AI can be dangerous in cybersecurity because it can lead to blind spots where organisations focus their defenses only against threats from certain countries while ignoring domestic or Western-origin attacks.
