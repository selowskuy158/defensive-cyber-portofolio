# B26 - Help Another Student in This Unit Struggling to Understand a Cybersecurity Concept

**Date:** 17 April, 2026
**Location:** UWA Campus, Perth

## Summary
This activity involves helping another student in CITS2006 who was struggling to understand a cybersecurity concept. I helped a classmate named (name kept private for privacy) who was having difficulty understanding how public key cryptography works, specifically the relationship between public keys and private keys and how they are used for encryption and digital signatures.

## Details

### The Problem
My classmate was confused about several aspects of public key cryptography. They did not understand why there are two keys, how one key can encrypt something that only the other key can decrypt, and why you would ever share a key publicly if it is supposed to be used for security. They also found it confusing that the same key pair is used differently depending on whether you are encrypting a message or creating a digital signature.

### How I Helped
I spent about an hour with my classmate at the UWA library explaining public key cryptography using a simple analogy. I compared the public key to a mailbox that anyone can drop a letter into, but only the person with the private key which is like the mailbox key can open it and read the letters inside. This helped them understand why the public key can be shared openly because knowing where the mailbox is does not help you read what is inside.

For digital signatures, I used a different analogy where I compared the private key to a personal stamp or seal that only the owner has. When you sign a document with your private key, anyone can use your public key to verify that the stamp is authentic, proving that the message came from you and was not altered. This helped my classmate understand why digital signatures use the private key for signing and the public key for verification, which is the opposite of encryption.

I also drew diagrams showing the flow of data in both encryption and signing scenarios, which my classmate said was really helpful because they are a visual learner. After our session, my classmate said they finally understood the concept and felt much more confident about it. We also practiced together by working through some example problems from the unit materials.

### What I Learned
Through helping my classmate, I realized that being able to explain complex technical concepts in simple terms is a very important skill. It also reinforced my own understanding of public key cryptography because teaching someone else forced me to think about the concept from different perspectives and make sure I truly understood every aspect of it.
