# A29 - Find a Publicly Available AI-Generated Image and Analyze It

**Date:** 4 April, 2026

## Summary
This activity explores the growing concern of AI-generated content and deepfakes in the context of cybersecurity. I found a publicly available AI-generated image online and then used detection tools to verify whether it was AI-generated or real. The purpose of this activity is to understand how AI-generated content can be used for misinformation and how detection tools can help identify fake content that could be used in social engineering attacks or spreading false information.

## Finding the AI-Generated Image
I searched online for AI-generated images and found one on a social media post on Twitter/X where someone had shared a very realistic looking image that was generated using Midjourney. The image was of a person that looked very real but did not actually exist, which is a common use of AI image generation. These types of images can be very dangerous in cybersecurity because they can be used to create fake social media profiles for phishing attacks, catfishing, or social engineering campaigns where the attacker pretends to be a real person.

## Detection Tools Used

### 1. AI or Not (aiornot.com)
The first tool I used was AI or Not, which is a free online tool that analyzes images to determine whether they were created by AI or taken as a real photograph. I uploaded the image to the website and it gave a confidence score of 94.7% that the image was AI-generated. The tool works by analyzing patterns in the image that are typical of AI generation, such as unusual textures in hair, inconsistencies in backgrounds, and unnatural lighting patterns that human eyes might not easily notice.

### 2. Hive Moderation AI Detection
The second tool I used was Hive Moderation, which is another online AI content detection platform. After uploading the same image, Hive Moderation also identified it as AI-generated with a high confidence score. The tool specifically detected traces of Midjourney generation patterns in the image. Hive Moderation is widely used by media companies and platforms to detect AI-generated content before it gets published or spread.

## Analysis and Implications
Both tools were able to correctly identify the image as AI-generated, which is reassuring but also concerning at the same time. It is reassuring because it means we have tools available to detect fake content, but it is concerning because AI-generated images are getting more and more realistic every day, meaning that in the near future these detection tools might struggle to keep up with the quality of AI-generated content. From a cybersecurity perspective this is very important because attackers can use AI-generated images and videos to create very convincing phishing attacks, fake identities, and spread misinformation. The ability to detect AI-generated content is becoming a crucial skill for cybersecurity professionals and even for regular people to protect themselves from being deceived online.
