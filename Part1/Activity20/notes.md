# A20 - Participate in a Discussion with Friends About Cybersecurity Event

**Date:** 18 March, 2026
**Location:** UWA Campus, Perth, Western Australia

## Summary
This activity explores the real-world perspectives of cybersecurity threats through discussion with classmates and friends, meaning that I got to hear different opinions and interpretations of a major cybersecurity incident. We met on campus and talked about the M&S ransomware attack that I had researched earlier, and the conversation really helped me understand how cybersecurity affects businesses and people in very different ways.

## The Discussion

**Who participated:** Around 5 of us from the CITS2006 unit met up near the student center on campus. We had different perspectives - some were more technical, some were thinking about business impacts, and others were concerned about personal data safety.

**What we discussed:** We started by talking about the Marks & Spencer attack that happened in April 2025, the same attack I had written about in Activity 15, where the Scattered Spider group used social engineering to compromise their systems and took them offline for 46 days.

## Different Perspectives We Discussed

**Social Engineering and Human Vulnerability:**
One of my friends, who works in IT support part-time, said that the hardest part of cybersecurity is the human element, meaning that no matter how good your technical defenses are, people can still fall for social engineering attacks. He explained that the M&S attack probably started with convincing someone to click a link or open a file, and once the attackers got inside, everything else followed. I noticed that everyone in the group agreed that this makes social engineering training very important.

**Third-Party Risk:**
Another person brought up that the attack highlighted how much risk companies face from third-party vendors and contractors, so meaning that even if your internal security is strong, attackers can come through supply chain vulnerabilities. They mentioned that many companies have lots of external vendors with access to their systems, and it's really hard to make sure all of them follow good security practices.

**Business Response and Recovery:**
We also talked about how M&S responded to the attack. Someone said that the 46-day downtime was devastating to their business, and that companies need to think about resilience and business continuity planning. I observed that the group had mixed opinions here - some thought M&S should have paid the ransom to get back up faster, but others said that would just encourage more attacks. We discussed the idea of having backups and recovery plans, so that when an attack happens, you can recover without paying criminals.

**Customer Trust and Reputation:**
One person raised a point about how this attack damaged M&S's reputation and customer trust, meaning that even though they recovered, many customers lost faith in their security. This led us to talk about whether companies should be transparent about breaches and how they affect customer confidence. I noticed that this was a concern for everyone in the group - even if you recover from an attack, the damage to your brand can last a long time.

**Prevention vs. Response:**
The discussion ended with us talking about whether it's better to spend money on prevention (like security training, intrusion detection, firewalls) or on response (like incident response teams, recovery plans, cyber insurance). The consensus was that both are needed, but many organizations prioritize response because prevention is harder to measure and justify to management.

## What I Learned

This discussion really helped me understand that cybersecurity is not just a technical problem - it's also about business decisions, customer relationships, employee behavior, and government regulations. The M&S attack is a good case study because it shows how a security incident affects so many different parts of a business. I also appreciated hearing different perspectives because it made me realize that there are many valid ways to think about these issues, and cybersecurity professionals need to understand business context, not just technical details.

## Conclusion

Participating in this informal discussion with friends and classmates was very valuable for developing a more rounded understanding of cybersecurity. Group discussions help identify gaps in knowledge and challenge assumptions, and I found that hearing other students' opinions made the abstract concept of "ransomware attack" feel much more real and relevant.
