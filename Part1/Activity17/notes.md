# A17 - Discover 10 Different Types of Locks in Use

**Date:** 2 April, 2026
**Location:** Various locations, Perth

## Summary
This activity explores different types of locks that are used in everyday life to secure physical spaces, belongings, and property. Locks are one of the oldest and most basic security mechanisms that people use to protect their assets and control access to certain areas. I observed and identified 10 different types of locks being used in various locations around Perth, including at home, on campus at UWA, and in public spaces.

## Types of Locks Discovered

### 1. Deadbolt Lock
Deadbolt locks are one of the most common types of locks used on the front doors of houses and residential properties. The way it works is by using a solid metal bolt that extends into the door frame when locked with a key or thumb turn from the inside. I observed this lock on the front door of my house in Mount Lawley. Deadbolts are considered very secure because they cannot be easily forced open compared to spring bolt locks.

### 2. Padlock
Padlocks are portable locks that can be used on gates, storage units, lockers, and chains. They come in different sizes and can use either a key or a combination to unlock. I noticed padlocks being used on the storage sheds at UWA campus and also on gym lockers. The advantage of padlocks is that they are removable and can be used on many different things.

### 3. Combination Lock
Combination locks use a sequence of numbers to unlock instead of a physical key. I found these being used on lockers at the UWA gym and also on some bicycle chains around campus. The way it works is by rotating the dial to the correct sequence of numbers, and then the lock opens. This is useful because there is no key to carry around or lose, but the downside is that someone could watch you enter the combination.

### 4. Smart Lock (Electronic)
Smart locks are modern electronic locks that can be unlocked using a smartphone app, fingerprint, or even voice command through a smart home system. I noticed that some newer apartment buildings around Perth CBD have smart locks installed on the entrance doors that residents can unlock using an app on their phone. This type of lock is very convenient because you do not need to carry a physical key and you can also give temporary access to visitors remotely.

### 5. Keycard Lock (RFID/NFC)
Keycard locks are commonly used in hotels and office buildings where a plastic card with an RFID or NFC chip is used to unlock the door by tapping on the reader. At UWA, many buildings use this exact system with the student card to access restricted areas like labs and study rooms. The way it works is the card sends a signal to the reader which verifies the card ID and then unlocks the door if the card is authorized.

### 6. Cam Lock (Mailbox Lock)
Cam locks are small cylindrical locks commonly found on mailboxes, filing cabinets, and vending machines. I observed this type of lock on my mailbox at home and also on the filing cabinets in the UWA library. The way it works is by inserting a small key and turning it, which rotates a metal piece called the cam that holds the door or drawer shut.

### 7. Sliding Bolt Lock
Sliding bolt locks are simple manual locks that work by sliding a metal bar into a bracket mounted on the door frame. I noticed these being used on bathroom doors in public restrooms and also on the inside of bedroom doors at home. They are very basic and do not require a key but they can only be locked and unlocked from one side, making them useful for privacy but not for security from the outside.

### 8. Chain Lock (Door Chain)
Chain locks are usually installed on the front doors of houses and apartments as an additional security layer. The way it works is that when the chain is engaged, the door can only be opened a few centimeters, allowing the person inside to see who is at the door without fully opening it. I observed this on the front door of my house and also on some older apartment doors in Perth. It provides an extra layer of security on top of the main lock.

### 9. Window Latch Lock
Window latch locks are small locks or clips that are installed on windows to prevent them from being opened from the outside. I observed these on the windows at home and also on the ground floor windows of some buildings at UWA campus. They are important because windows are a common entry point for burglars, and without a proper latch lock, the window can be easily slid open from outside.

### 10. Bike Cable Lock
Bike cable locks are flexible steel cable locks that cyclists use to secure their bikes to a fixed object like a bike rack or pole. I noticed many students at UWA using cable locks to secure their bikes at the bike racks near the library and lecture halls. The way it works is by wrapping the cable around the bike frame and the rack, then locking it with either a key or combination. Although cable locks are convenient and lightweight, they are not the most secure because they can be cut with bolt cutters, so many cyclists also use a U-lock for extra protection.
